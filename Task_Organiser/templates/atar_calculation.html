How ATAR is Calculated

Overview

The Australian Tertiary Admission Rank (ATAR) is a percentile ranking used by universities to select students for undergraduate courses. Our calculator estimates your ATAR using publicly available UAC scaling data and aggregation rules.

The calculation follows four steps: enter HSC marks → scale each subject → select your best 10 units (English mandatory) → convert the aggregate to an ATAR.

Step 1: HSC Marks

Input: The HSC mark NESA reports to you (0–100 for standard subjects; 0–50 for most extension subjects).

Normalisation: Extension marks out of 50 are converted to the 0–100 scale used by scaling tables before scaling (e.g. 35/50 → 70).

Example: English Advanced: 85/100, Mathematics Extension 1: 35/50

Step 2: Subject Scaling

What is scaling? UAC adjusts marks so performance can be compared across subjects of different difficulty.

Our method: Piecewise linear interpolation between published (HSC mark, scaled mark) pairs from hscscalinggraphs.au and matrix.edu.au, covering {{ subject_count }} subjects.

Every published data point is matched exactly; values between points are linearly interpolated.

Note: Scaling varies across the mark range. A subject that scales up at HSC 75 may behave differently at HSC 90.

Step 3: Aggregate Score

What is the aggregate? The sum of scaled marks from your best 10 units.

Aggregation rules:
  • English mandatory: At least 2 units of English must be included when available
  • Best 10 units: Remaining places filled by highest scaled units
  • Unit counts: Extension subjects count as 1 unit; standard subjects count as 2 units
  • Maximum possible aggregate: 1000.0 (10 units × 100.0 scaled mark per unit)

Step 4: ATAR Conversion

Final step: Map the aggregate score to an ATAR using UAC's published conversion table with linear interpolation between breakpoints.

Rounding: The estimated ATAR is rounded to the nearest 0.05, matching UAC reporting (e.g. 90.00, 79.95).

Technical Details

Scaling implementation
  • Data: hscscalinggraphs.au curve readings (2025) plus matrix.edu.au anchor points at HSC 20 and HSC 75
  • Interpolation: numpy.interp between sorted published pairs
  • Below-range marks: Linear scaling from the origin through the lowest published anchor
  • Above-range marks: Capped at the highest published scaled mark
ATAR conversion
  • Linear interpolation across UAC aggregate-to-ATAR breakpoints
  • Final ATAR rounded to the nearest 0.05 (maximum 99.95)
Data sources
  • hscscalinggraphs.au — HSC mark vs scaled mark curves per subject
  • matrix.edu.au — Exact anchor pairs across mark levels
  • UAC published tables — Aggregate-to-ATAR conversion breakpoints
Accuracy considerations
  • Based on publicly available data; actual UAC methodology is proprietary
  • Scaling curves change year to year with cohort performance
  • School moderation affects HSC marks before scaling is applied
Calculator limitations

This calculator provides estimates only and should not be used for official purposes:

  • Does not replicate UAC's proprietary algorithm exactly
  • Based on published historical data, not the current year's final scaling
  • Cannot predict moderation or assessment performance changes
  • Some subjects in the app (e.g. IPT) may not yet have scaling data
